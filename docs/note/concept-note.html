Dag দাগ: a clearance gate for land registration

Stopping a bad deed before it is signed, instead of arguing about it for nine years.

1. The problem we picked

In Bangladesh a deed is registered at the sub-registry office under the Ministry of Law. The record of ownership is corrected at the AC (Land) office under the Ministry of Land. These are two ministries, two databases and two queues, and a deed registered in one of them does not necessarily reach the other [1]. The buyer walks out holding paper that the state does not yet recognise.

The government has already attacked half of this. e-Mutation cut the namjari process from about 60 days to 28 and from three or four office visits to one [2]. A registration-mutation interconnection was inaugurated in March 2023 and runs in a set of pilot upazilas [3]. On 1 January 2026 the Registration (Amendment) Ordinance amended the 1908 Act and opened the legal route for e-registration through government-approved software [4].

So the pipe between the two offices is being built. What nobody has built is the gate. Nothing today checks, at the counter, whether the deed about to be registered should be registered at all. That is the gap we are filling.

2. Why it matters here

Roughly two million land cases sit with the judiciary, more than 70 percent of all litigation in the country [5]. The Senior Secretary of the Ministry of Land has put the figure near 80 percent [6]. One household in seven is in a land dispute, the average case runs about eight years, and some have run forty [5].

Most of these cases are not legal puzzles. They are bad entries that nobody checked. A single Prothom Alo investigation from Faridpur shows the whole chain in one story: after a landowner died, people claiming to be his heirs were brought in from across the border on forged certificates, the land was registered in their name, and almost immediately afterwards a third party used the same land as collateral for a bank loan [7].

That one case is the argument for both of our additions. An alert to the real family would have exposed the registration within minutes. A charge check at the counter would have exposed the loan.

3. What we are building

Dag is a verification service that sits in front of registration. The sub-registrar enters the mouza, the dag number, the seller, the buyer and the share being sold. Dag runs a fixed set of checks and stamps the request PASS, WARN, HELD or BLOCKED, with every reason and its evidence. A pass, or a warning the officer accepts with a written note, produces a signed clearance with a QR code that anyone can scan to confirm it is real, on the phone itself.

When the deed is executed, Dag runs the checks once more and voids the clearance if anything changed in between. Then it emits one event. That event opens the namjari case at the AC (Land) office with no application from the citizen, writes the transferred share into the ledger, and texts the owner.

The name comes from the dag number, because the plot, not the person and not the document, is the only thing every office in the chain agrees on.

The seven base checks

C1 Seller in record. Seller NID matches a current khatian holder; a different name on the deed raises a warning.

C2 Area ledger. Shares already transferred out of the dag must not exceed the recorded area. This is what catches a 12-decimal plot being sold three times at 10 decimals.

C3 Pending deed lock. A deed in flight locks the dag, so two offices cannot register the same plot in the same week.

C4 Khajna and mutation. Tax paid, and the seller holds a mutation khatian in his own name.

C5 Government interest. Khas, acquired, waqf, debottar, vested.

C6 Court injunction. Any pending suit recorded against the dag.

C7 Survey chain. CS to SA to RS to BS continuity for the dag, with a flag where the chain breaks.

Every block carries a reason code, the evidence and an appeal number. We added the appeal route deliberately. In Telangana, where registration is blocked automatically against a prohibited list, migration errors put thousands of legitimate private plots on that list and owners only discovered it at the counter [8]. A gate without an appeal path becomes a new kind of injustice.

4. The two additions (P6)

4.1 Owner Lock and Owner Alert (C8)

An owner registers his dag against his NID and mobile number. From then on, any deed, mutation or mortgage touching that dag sends him an SMS in Bangla and English within seconds. He can also lock the dag. A locked dag does not block a sale outright: the request is held and the owner gets a 48-hour notice on his own login with two buttons, "yes, it is me" and "no, not me". Yes lets the deed through; no blocks it; silence after 48 hours turns into a warning that the officer must answer in writing, with a fingerprint.

This is ordinary practice in United States county recorder offices and is now being made compulsory. Fifteen states require recorders to run owner alert systems, verify identity, or intervene when a suspicious document is filed; in Texas the recorder must notify the last known owner and refer the document to the district attorney [9]. From 1 January 2027 every California county must notify the owner whenever a deed or mortgage is recorded against his property [10]. Leavenworth County in Kansas and Platte County in Missouri go further and let an owner freeze his own deed [9].

Bangladesh has nothing of the kind. Every existing service is built around the buyer and the office. Nobody is watching on behalf of the person who already owns the land, which is exactly why a man working abroad, or a family that has just buried someone, finds out far too late. In Moulvibazar a Bangladesh Bank officer described losing control of family debutter property to forged signatures and a forged power of attorney, and said he could not even pay his land tax because false deeds had been filed at the land office [11].

4.2 Encumbrance oracle (C9)

Before registration, Dag asks the banking side one question: is there a live charge on this dag? It gets back yes or no, the date the charge was created, and the lender's name. Nothing else.

India solved this in 2011 with CERSAI, a central registry of security interests created so that a second lender can see the first lender's charge before granting a loan; the public can search it for ten rupees [12]. Bangladesh has the data but not the access. Bangladesh Bank's Credit Information Bureau runs a Collateral Information System, and since 2018 banks have been required to pull a collateral report before accepting a mortgage [13]. It is closed to citizens and to sub-registrars. The cost of that is visible: REHAB had received around 340 complaints of hidden developer mortgages by September last year, involving close to Tk 1,500 crore of buyers' money, and said openly that the country lacks a central digital registry of mortgaged property [14].

Our contribution is not the registry. It is the yes/no oracle. Bangladesh Bank cannot open CIB records to the public without amending the Bangladesh Bank Order [15], so a design that demands open data is a design that never ships. A signed yes/no discloses one fact, leaks no borrower information, and needs no change in the law. The same oracle serves a bank's loan desk, so a second loan against a pledged plot is refused before any money moves, which is the job CERSAI does in India. Every reply is logged with what went out and what stayed inside the bank.

9. The prototype and its tech stack

The same backend code runs in three places. A Node server on Render holds the API; the front end on Vercel calls it across origins; and if the free Render service is asleep for more than 80 seconds, the page runs the same backend inside the browser, so a judge never meets a broken screen. Each browser gets its own workspace on the server, so judges sharing one link never see each other's clicks. The demo has five role logins (sub-registrar, landowner, AC Land, bank officer, buyer) with role checks on every route, salted and stretched password hashes, a lockout after five wrong tries, and 12-hour sessions.

Clearances are signed with Ed25519 (TweetNaCl) over canonical JSON, with secret keys on the server. The QR code carries a short signed token with only the last four NID digits, verified on the buyer's phone with the public key. The audit log is a SHA-512 hash chain. Every screen, SMS, error and guide step exists in English and Bangla, with Bangla digits, lakh grouping and Dhaka time. Thirty-four automated tests, three of them over real HTTP, run on every push. The demo page shows each API call live, with the steps inside the gate. We are not using a blockchain. The threat here is bad data entering a trusted registry, and a distributed ledger does not check whether a seller owns the land. A rules engine does.

10. Nationwide applicability

The gate is a service, not a replacement system. It sits between offices that already exist and speaks the identifiers those offices already use, so it can be switched on one upazila at a time, in read-only advisory mode first, and the reconciliation report alone gives the AC (Land) office a worklist on day one. Punjab in Pakistan ran this play and brought property registration down from 56 days to 11 across 55 million landowner records [16]. Karnataka made a verified digital record compulsory before registration, and revenue officials report that unauthorised registrations and the same-plot-sold-twice pattern stopped, while genuine registrations did not fall [17].

Beyond registration, the same data answers the questions the brief lists for other offices: banks checking collateral, buyers checking a plot on the mouza map before they pay, acquisition offices identifying rightful owners before paying compensation, and courts needing a dated ownership history.

What we are not claiming

We did not invent the registration-to-mutation link; the government is already building it [3]. We are not proposing conclusive title. Mutation remains evidentiary, not determinative of ownership. And the charge oracle depends on a cooperation that does not exist yet. We have built our side of it and mocked the other side honestly, and the README says so.
